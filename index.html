FIELD GUIDE

The signal needs a second look.

The water keeps moving while this guide is open.

Read before you commit
Select a message or a location on the map. Drag a unit category onto a street or report. Or select a category, choose a destination, and press Send. All assistant functions start off. Expand its original source. District labels, sensor limitations, and source IDs are clues.
Trust / verify / override
With map reports enabled, dispatch uses the AI assessment. With it off, dispatch uses the direct source location. A drone takes 5 seconds to reach the site, 15 to scan, and 5 to return. It reveals field evidence at the end of its scan. Correct lets you change sector and priority; then dispatch to act. Archive a report you judge empty or duplicated.
Capacity and movement
Ground teams carry 8 people and can clear collisions, bridge approaches, and traffic jams. Skiffs follow open corridors and carry up to 16 people. The airlift crosses terrain and carries 12. Select a reception hub with space. A dispatch reserves one full load until the unit returns.
Broadcast evacuation
Select a sector and a hub, then broadcast its route. Walking residents leave in groups of four rather than one. Rescue units are still needed for trapped residents.
Assistant controls
Map fusion adds AI markers. Noise filter focuses Radio on the selected unit. For rescue boats, it keeps only waterfront people, explicit water incidents, and water-specific operations; inland calls and general traffic are hidden. Route advice draws suggested paths. Call summaries simplify incoming calls; original sources remain available in Source streams.
Changing conditions
The shift moves from clear conditions to blind spots, then a relay outage and recovery. A restored relay does not mean every source is perfect. Compare independent evidence.

The scenario and AI are scripted. This is a fictional decision game, not an emergency procedure.
